# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of timely technology news for readers who follow the technology industry and its interaction with markets. Prioritize developments that change competitive position, capital spending, pricing, supply chains, regulation, or the economics of AI and semiconductors, while still covering notable AI tools and open-source releases.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Groundbreaking.** Major breakthroughs, paradigm shifts, major versions of widely used technology, significant research results, or industry-changing announcements with clear commercial or strategic consequences.
- **7-8: High value.** Important developments worth prompt attention, including technical deep-dives, novel approaches, and valuable tools or libraries that materially change what teams can build or buy.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Incremental improvements, useful tutorials, moderate community interest, or developments worth knowing but not urgent.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine updates, minor releases, common knowledge, shallow treatment, or content dominated by promotion.
- **0-2: Noise.** Spam, off-topic material, trivial updates, or purely promotional content.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider how many users, businesses, or investors are affected; whether the change alters competition, costs, capital spending, or regulation; source quality; and concrete supporting details. Pure tutorials, minor library updates, and low-level engineering detail are lower priority unless they change what teams can build or buy. Treat substantive community debate as additional evidence of value, but do not equate popularity with importance. Do not reward exaggerated headlines.

Use three to five specific topic tags.

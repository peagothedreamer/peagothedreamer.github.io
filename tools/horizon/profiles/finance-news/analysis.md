# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether an item gives a reader who follows the US economy and US equities timely, credible, and materially useful information. Judge importance by likely effect on interest rates, earnings expectations, valuations, sector leadership, or economic policy—not by how much attention it receives.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** A Federal Reserve policy decision or emergency action, a major inflation or employment surprise, a landmark election or war outcome, severe market disruption, a sweeping tariff or regulatory regime, or mega-cap earnings that reprice an entire sector.
- **7-8: Important.** Material earnings surprises or guidance changes, consequential economic data, sector-wide policy or regulation, major M&A, IPO, or financing activity, or a significant court or regulatory ruling with clear economic effects.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible, concrete developments that help explain a company, market, or economic trend but have limited breadth or urgency.
- **3-4: Low value.** Routine daily price moves, expected results, small transactions, forecasts without a meaningful baseline, or coverage that merely repeats a headline.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumors, promotional investment claims, unexplained numbers, sensational predictions, or content with no reliable financial substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Adopt a US investor's perspective. Consider magnitude, breadth, source quality, timeliness, the affected groups, and whether the item provides concrete figures with meaningful baselines. Distinguish reported facts from forecasts, proposals, and opinions. A large percentage move is not important without considering the starting value. Do not treat market popularity as economic importance. Score political and geopolitical items by their concrete economic or policy consequences, even if markets have not yet reacted.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
